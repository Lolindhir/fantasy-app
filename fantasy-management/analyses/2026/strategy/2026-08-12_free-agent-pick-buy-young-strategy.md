# 2026 Free-Agent-Pick Buy-Young Strategy

Date: 2026-08-12
Status: Working analysis / historical decision context
Perspective: Mighty Giants (TeamID 1)

## Purpose

This document stores the current strategic view for converting 2026 Free Agent Draft picks into young dynasty assets.

It is deliberately a dated analysis, not a permanent rule and not a binding decision. Dynamic player roles, injuries, rankings, market values and the actual free-agent pool must be refreshed before any concrete trade is accepted.

## Current Mighty Giants context

Mighty Giants remains a contender with unusually strong depth across QB, RB, WR and TE in a 6-team, PPR, 2QB, 2TE, 4Flex league.

The roster already contains premium and high-end assets such as Patrick Mahomes, Jayden Daniels, Breece Hall, Saquon Barkley, Kenneth Walker, Puka Nacua, Malik Nabers, Marvin Harrison Jr., Ladd McConkey, Jaylen Waddle, Trey McBride, Tyler Warren and Colston Loveland.

Strategic implication:

- prioritize quality and consolidation over generic depth;
- do not pay generic Superflex scarcity premiums when league replacement is deep;
- young WR upside is especially useful because it can create either future lineup value or liquid trade value;
- every trade involving a Free Agent Draft pick must be compared with the actual expected free-agent shelf at that exact overall pick.

## Core opportunity-cost model

A Free Agent Draft pick must not be valued as if it were a same-numbered Rookie Draft pick.

The relevant question is:

> Is the player we receive clearly better for Mighty Giants than the actual fantasy free-agent shelf we are likely to have available at this exact overall selection?

The current rough market-based free-agent shelf, using actual league ownership exclusions and dated external market context, was estimated as follows:

- 1.01: Tetairoa McMillan tier, with Brock Purdy also available in the broad top range
- 2.04: approximately Parker Washington tier
- 3.04: approximately Chuba Hubbard / J.K. Dobbins / Jalen Coker tier depending on QB devaluation and opponent behavior
- 4.04: approximately AJ Barner tier
- 5.04: deep young stash range such as Gunnar Helm / Zachariah Branch-type profiles depending on prior picks

These are opportunity-cost anchors, not a deterministic draft forecast.

## Pick hierarchy

### 1.01

Default target: Tetairoa McMillan.

This pick should be strongly protected. It should only be moved for a clearly superior elite asset or for a T-Mac-adjacent young asset plus meaningful additional value.

Do not trade 1.01 for quantity.

### 2.04

This is materially stronger than a generic “second-round” label implies because the true fantasy free-agent pool remains deep.

Do not spend 2.04 on merely interesting young upside.

A target should either:

- have clearly higher ceiling and liquidity than the likely 2.04 shelf; or
- be close in neutral market value but offer clearly better Mighty Giants roster fit.

### 3.04

This is currently the most attractive conversion zone for young WR upside.

The likely free-agent shelf still has playable value, but several likely options are at positions where Mighty Giants already has substantial depth. Therefore converting 3.04 into a young WR with a credible NFL role path can be positive roster-specific arbitrage.

### 4.04

Another strong conversion pick, particularly when the expected free-agent shelf is TE- or replaceable-depth-heavy.

### 5.04

Mostly a sweetener, but not worthless. Deep young stashes can still remain. Do not attach it automatically if the trade is already fair.

### 2. Jayden Higgins — Tim / TeamID 6

Why target:

- age 23.7;
- Year 2;
- nominal Houston WR2 in current repo data;
- rookie season: 119.2 fantasy points, 7.45 per game, 16 of 16 games;
- current camp/offseason reporting has been positive regarding strength, confidence, chemistry and red-zone involvement.

Important context / caveat:

Higgins' rookie production must not be treated as if he had already beaten a fully healthy Houston WR room.

Tank Dell missed the entire 2025 season, which materially opened role and target opportunity behind Nico Collins. Nico Collins was present for most of the regular season, so Higgins did not primarily benefit from a long Collins absence. The larger structural tailwind was Dell being unavailable for the full year.

Therefore the correct interpretation is:

- Higgins showed useful rookie competence and durability;
- but some of the opportunity was injury-created;
- 2026 is a more meaningful test of whether he can retain or expand his role once Dell is available again.

Price framework after this caveat:

- Wunschpreis: 4.04 + 5.04
- Fair: 3.04 straight
- Schmerzgrenze: 3.04 straight under current information
- 3.04 + 5.04: currently no; reconsider only if clearer 2026 WR2 usage emerges
- 2.04: no

Conviction: medium-high, lower than the first pass before the Dell context was explicitly incorporated.

## Current strategic conclusion

The preferred overall approach is:

- protect 1.01 unless consolidating into clearly elite young value;
- protect 2.04 from mediocre upside trades;
- actively use 3.04 and 4.04 to seek young WR upside where the target clearly beats the likely league-specific free-agent replacement shelf;
- use 5.04 selectively as a sweetener, not automatically;
- always price the player against the exact FA-pick opportunity cost rather than the label of the pick.

The opposing manager must beat the user's own draft alternative.
